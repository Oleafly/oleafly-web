# arXiv and the preprint world

Somewhere in your first week around research, someone will say "it's on arXiv" and everyone will nod. Here is what they mean.

## First, what a preprint is

A **preprint** is a research paper shared publicly *before* (or while) it goes through peer review. No gatekeeper has verified it yet. It is the author saying "here is our work, read it now" instead of waiting the months that [peer review](/learn/peer-review-explained/) takes.

## arXiv, pronounced "archive"

**arXiv** (arxiv.org) is the giant free preprint server used by physics, math, computer science, and neighbors. The X is a Greek chi, so you say it "archive." It started in 1991 at Los Alamos and now hosts millions of papers. Anyone can read everything, free, forever. Uploading requires only a light endorsement check, not review.

Every paper gets an **arXiv ID** like `2401.12345`: the first four digits are year and month (January 2024), the rest is a sequence number. Versions are tracked too, so `2401.12345v2` is the second revision. That ID is a permanent address. Paste it into a search bar or a citation tool and the paper comes up. Oleafly, for instance, can fetch a full BibTeX entry from just an arXiv ID or DOI when you are [adding citations](/learn/add-citations/).

## Why people post preprints

- **Speed.** Your result is public today, not in eight months.
- **Priority.** The timestamp proves you had the idea first.
- **Feedback.** Readers email you problems before reviewers do.
- **Access.** Anyone can read it, no paywall, see [open access, paywalls, and DOIs](/learn/open-access-doi-explained/).
- **Visibility.** In fast-moving fields like machine learning, people read arXiv daily. The "published" version months later is almost an afterthought.

In much of CS and physics, posting to arXiv the same week you submit to a venue is simply standard practice. One caution: a few journals and double-blind venues have rules about preprints, so check before posting.

## The cousins

| Server | Fields |
| --- | --- |
| arXiv | Physics, math, CS, stats, quantitative biology and finance |
| bioRxiv | Biology (say "bio-archive") |
| medRxiv | Medicine and health sciences |
| SSRN | Social sciences, economics, law |
| ChemRxiv | Chemistry |

Same idea everywhere: free, fast, unreviewed.

## Preprint vs published version

The preprint and the eventual published paper are usually near-twins with different clothes. The published (or "camera-ready") version has survived review, incorporated fixes, and wears the venue's official formatting. Authors often update the arXiv version to match after acceptance, so the free copy stays current. When both exist, cite the published version; it is the version of record.

## "Can I cite a preprint?"

Yes, and people do constantly, especially for recent work. Just know what the citation carries: a preprint says "this exists and claims X," not "experts verified X." For anything load-bearing in your own paper, prefer peer-reviewed sources when they exist, and treat bold preprint-only claims with friendly suspicion. Citing mechanics are covered in [add citations](/learn/add-citations/) and [create a bibliography](/learn/create-bibliography/).

## Where this fits
